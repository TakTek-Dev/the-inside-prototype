The Arab vote

Turnout decides how many seats the two lists win, and whether the change bloc can reach 61.

Stat-Net forecasts 63% Arab turnout, against about 53% in 2022. In Maariv’s poll 81% of Arab-party supporters said they would surely vote, up from 74% on 8 October.

Protest · Arraba, 3 October

Twenty-six years on, the march still starts at the graves

On 3 October Arab towns marked the anniversary of October 2000, when thirteen men were killed by police, twelve of them Arab citizens. The High Follow-Up Committee led a route through the graves in Jatt, Umm al-Fahm, Muawiya, Nazareth, Kafr Kanna, Kafr Manda and Sakhnin.

The march left Arraba’s old mosque on Daher al-Omar Street at four in the afternoon and ended at the memorial in the school square. Earlier this year the street kept its own count: in January a shopkeepers’ strike in Sakhnin against protection rackets grew into a march of tens of thousands in Tel Aviv.

Bokra, 3 October 2026; Times of Israel, 5 February 2026.

Method

Whose count, and why one

We keep the Abraham Initiatives’ tally. Police figures count murder cases, not people, and other monitors count differently. When they disagree we name the source; we never average.

Counts change after deaths in hospital: we correct the ledger, never overwrite it.

News of Palestinian citizens of Israel, the people the Arab press calls the Inside, kept as a record: every story entered against the count, every figure sourced, every correction left legible.
